# Customer research

> **Status:** reviewed practical guide. Interview evidence is self-report and must not be presented as proof of demand.

Interview for past behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm. Ask the person to walk through the last time the problem occurred: trigger, steps, tools, people involved, time, cost, failure points and workaround.

## Useful questions
- “Tell me about the last time you had to do this.”
- “What happened immediately before and after?”
- “What did you try? What did it cost in time or money?”
- “How often does this happen? Who else is involved?”
- “What happens if nothing changes?”
- “Have you paid for or built a workaround?”

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use my app?” Ask permission before recording. Protect personal and commercially sensitive information.

## Recruit for relevant experience

Define the role, situation, and recency that make a participant informative. Recruit beyond friends and people already enthusiastic about the idea when possible. In business-to-business settings, distinguish users, buyers, approvers, security or procurement reviewers, and affected third parties. Record how participants were recruited because convenience samples skew findings.

Do not promise confidentiality you cannot provide. Explain the purpose, whether notes or recordings will be kept, who can access them, how long they will be retained, and how a participant can decline or stop. Collect only what is needed; avoid sensitive personal data unless there is a clear, lawful, safeguarded reason.

## Run and synthesize an interview

1. Ask for a recent concrete instance and let the participant reconstruct the sequence.
2. Probe for artifacts or observable details where appropriate: a form, handoff, delay, workaround, or decision point. Do not request confidential records.
3. Separate verbatim notes or direct observations from your interpretation.
4. After the conversation, capture the workflow, frequency, consequences, existing alternatives, surprises, and unanswered questions.
5. Compare patterns across participants and preserve contradictions. A vivid quote is not a prevalence estimate.

Use the [customer interview template](../../templates/CUSTOMER_INTERVIEW.md). Avoid writing identifying details into shared notes unless essential and consented to.

### Limits and next evidence

Interviews can reveal context, vocabulary, and past behavior. They are vulnerable to recall error, social desirability, selection bias, and small or unrepresentative samples. Statements about future use or willingness to pay are weaker than observed choices with real consequences. Follow a promising interview signal with observation, a prototype task, a service pilot, or another experiment that tests the specific uncertainty.
