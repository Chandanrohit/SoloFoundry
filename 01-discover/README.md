# Discover: opportunity intelligence

> **Status:** reviewed learning path; individual guides identify their own maturity and evidence limits.

The first job is not to generate ideas. It is to discover a meaningful, recurring problem and gather enough evidence to decide whether solving it is worthwhile.

## Discovery loop
1. Collect problem signals: complaints, workarounds, repetitive tasks, delays, errors, regulation or technology shifts.
2. Identify the people and workflow affected.
3. Interview and observe without pitching a solution.
4. Quantify frequency, severity, cost and consequences.
5. Map alternatives, competitors and the status quo.
6. Test risky assumptions with the cheapest credible experiment.
7. Decide: build, run another test, pivot, pause or reject.

## A practical learning path

1. [Problem discovery](problem-discovery/README.md): collect and qualify problem signals.
2. [Customer research](customer-research/README.md): recruit, interview, and document what people actually do.
3. [Jobs and workflows](jobs-and-workflows/README.md): describe the job, workflow, actors, and failure points without prematurely designing a solution.
4. [Market intelligence](market-intelligence/README.md): map buyers, substitutes, reachable segments, and market-size assumptions.
5. [Assumptions and evidence](assumptions-and-evidence/README.md): identify what must be true and what is still unknown.
6. [Validation](validation/README.md): design a proportionate experiment and set a decision rule before running it.
7. [Synthesis and decision](synthesis-and-decisions/README.md): update beliefs and decide to build, test, pivot, pause, or reject.

Use the [worked example](worked-example/README.md) to see how the steps fit together. It is an explicitly hypothetical teaching case, not customer research or market evidence.

## Methods and frameworks
Jobs to Be Done, Customer Development, Design Thinking, Lean Startup, Value Proposition Canvas, Business Model Canvas, Opportunity Solution Trees, assumption mapping, TAM/SAM/SOM, Porter's Five Forces and SWOT are lenses—not substitutes for customer evidence.

Frameworks are prompts for inquiry, not validated instruments by themselves. Prefer direct behavioral evidence and disclose uncertainty. The references at the end of each guide explain where guidance comes from.
