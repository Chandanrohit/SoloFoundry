# Assumptions and evidence

> **Status:** reviewed decision aid. Evidence strength depends on how a claim was tested and in what context.

## Make uncertainty visible

For each major claim, record: what must be true, who or what it concerns, why failure matters, current evidence, source and date, uncertainty, and the next practical test. Separate facts reported or observed, interpretations, assumptions, and decisions. An assumption is not a fact because it appears in a canvas or spreadsheet.

## Prioritize what to test

Start with claims that could invalidate the opportunity: problem severity and recurrence, access to the affected people, a reachable buyer or user, a viable path to adoption, feasibility, and any safety, legal, data, or trust constraint. Mark each by consequence if false and how little is known. The resulting order is a discussion aid, not an objective probability score.

## Read evidence in context

Evidence is not a single ladder that makes every number comparable. Ask:

- Is this a direct observation, self-report, intention, or action with a real consequence?
- Was the participant representative of the intended user, buyer, or context?
- Was the behavior spontaneous, prompted, incentivized, or required by the test?
- What denominator, time window, comparison, and alternative explanation apply?
- Could selection, measurement, novelty, seasonality, or the researcher's presence explain the result?
- What independent source or method could corroborate it?

A paid pilot is stronger evidence of a specific buyer's commitment than a positive interview answer, but it does not by itself prove repeatability, retention, margins, or a larger market. A survey can estimate responses in a defined sample; it cannot repair a poorly defined population or leading questions.

## Update rather than defend

State what observation would increase or decrease confidence before the test. Preserve negative and mixed results. Update the claim narrowly: one segment's response under one set of conditions does not validate a universal market claim.
