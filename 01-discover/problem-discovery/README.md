# Problem discovery

> **Status:** reviewed synthesis of discovery practice; examples are illustrative and local observations must be gathered for each opportunity.

## Where to look
- Repeated manual work, spreadsheet glue, copy/paste workflows and handoffs.
- Complaints in communities, support forums, app reviews and professional groups.
- Job descriptions that reveal expensive recurring work.
- Workarounds, shadow IT, errors, delays and compliance burdens.
- New regulations, infrastructure shifts or model capabilities that change feasibility.

## Capture a problem signal
Record the source, date, affected role, context, exact observed behavior, current workaround, frequency, consequence, confidence and follow-up question. A complaint is a signal—not proof of a market.

## Avoid
- Starting with a technology looking for a use case.
- Treating likes, survey enthusiasm or competitor existence as willingness to pay.
- Confusing a feature request with the underlying job or pain.
- Assuming a large TAM proves reachable demand.

Use [Problem Brief](../../templates/PROBLEM_BRIEF.md) and [Opportunity Assessment](../../templates/OPPORTUNITY_ASSESSMENT.md).

## Rank signals without pretending to measure a market

For each signal, record its source and context, then ask whether it is recent, repeated, costly, associated with a clear actor and workflow, and corroborated independently. These dimensions help choose what to investigate next; they are not a validated scoring model. Do not total them into a claim of market size.

Look for disconfirming cases: people who do not experience the problem, users who solve it easily, or contexts where the workaround is good enough. Ask who bears the cost and who can authorize a change; these may be different people.
