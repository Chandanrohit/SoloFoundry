# Synthesis and decisions

> **Status:** reviewed decision guide. The decision categories are prompts for deliberate action, not a formula that removes judgment.

## Synthesize without flattening disagreement

After a research round, summarize recurring behaviors, important differences, counterexamples, and unknowns. Trace each conclusion to its evidence and note sample and method limits. Keep direct observations separate from your explanation. Do not count repeated statements as independent corroboration when participants share the same source, employer, or incentive.

## Choose the next action

- **Build a narrow test or product slice** when the problem and user are sufficiently clear for a bounded implementation test and the remaining risk is best learned through use.
- **Test again** when a consequential assumption remains uncertain and a proportionate experiment can change the decision.
- **Pivot or re-segment** when evidence points to a different user, buyer, workflow, or outcome than the original hypothesis.
- **Pause** when a dependency, access constraint, safety question, or external condition prevents an informative test now. State what would restart the work.
- **Reject** when evidence repeatedly contradicts a critical premise or the risks and costs exceed the plausible value. Record what was learned so the same premise is not silently recycled.

Before choosing, ask what evidence would change your mind, whether the test is ethically and operationally acceptable, and what a false positive or false negative would cost. Record the decision, rationale, confidence, dissent, and next review trigger in the opportunity assessment or an ADR.

## Do not mistake a score for a decision

Scores help reveal disagreement and missing evidence; totals can hide a fatal constraint and imply precision the inputs do not support. Use ranges, plain-language confidence, and explicit assumptions. Revisit the decision when material evidence changes.
