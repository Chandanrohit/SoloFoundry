# Market intelligence

> **Status:** reviewed method overview. No market figures or segment estimates are supplied in this guide.

Study customers, budgets, substitutes, competitors, channels and structural constraints. Competitors include manual processes, spreadsheets, internal tools, agencies and doing nothing.

## Market sizing
- TAM: total theoretical demand under clearly stated assumptions.
- SAM: portion reachable with the product, geography, segment and business model.
- SOM: realistic obtainable share over a stated period, constrained by distribution and execution.

Prefer bottom-up estimates from customer counts × plausible spend, then triangulate with credible top-down sources. Show assumptions and ranges; never present speculative precision.

## Competitive analysis
Compare target segment, job solved, workflow, pricing model, distribution, trust, integrations, switching costs, strengths, weaknesses and unmet needs. Include substitutes and “do nothing.”

## Define a reachable segment

Describe users and buyers by the situation and workflow relevant to the problem, then identify how to find and contact them. State geography, organization type, size, role, current tools, and exclusions where they affect adoption. Distinguish the person using a product from the person who pays, approves, implements, or bears risk.

## Make estimates auditable

Use a bottom-up model when you can: count a defined population from a traceable source, estimate the share with the relevant workflow using evidence, and apply a plausible annual spend or revenue capture. Show each input, date, geography, range, and uncertainty. Do not multiply a global industry report by an arbitrary market share and call the result obtainable revenue. TAM, SAM, and SOM depend on definitions; label the method and time horizon.

Competitor research is a map of alternatives and positioning at a point in time. Record when and where pricing or product claims were checked. Public competitor information can be incomplete or stale; do not infer customer satisfaction or revenue from marketing pages alone.
