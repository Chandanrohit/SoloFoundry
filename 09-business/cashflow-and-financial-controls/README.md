# Cash flow and financial controls

**Maturity:** reviewed method guide; examples are illustrative and not accounting advice.

Profit and cash are different. A solo business can show revenue while running short of cash because payments arrive later, annual bills are due, refunds occur, or taxes and owner withdrawals are not reserved. Maintain separate personal and business records where appropriate and confirm local requirements with a qualified accountant.

## Build a simple monthly view

Track at least:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Cash in | Collected sales, refunds reversed, financing, owner contributions |
| Variable out | Payment fees, usage-based hosting/API, support contractors, refunds |
| Fixed/periodic out | Hosting minimums, software, insurance, accounting, domain renewals |
| Obligations | Taxes, refunds, chargebacks, debt, annual prepayments, customer credits |
| Owner cash | Compensation/distributions, clearly separated from operating expense where accounting rules require |

For each line record date, amount, currency, source, recurring/one-time status, and confidence. Reconcile processor deposits against gross sales, fees, refunds, and tax withholding; a payout is not the same as gross revenue.

## Forecast runway with scenarios

Start with current unrestricted cash and a 13-week forecast. Each week record expected receipts and committed payments; use conservative dates for uncertain invoices. Then make a 12-month monthly low/base/high view for seasonality, renewals, taxes, and planned spend.

`ending cash = starting cash + cash received − cash paid`

Runway is a rough planning measure, not a promise. If monthly net burn is stable, `cash ÷ monthly burn` gives a quick estimate, but it ignores timing and changing obligations. Show the assumptions and an action threshold (for example, freeze discretionary spend or seek advice when forecast cash falls below a defined reserve).

## Lightweight controls for one person

- Use a dedicated business account where feasible and allowed; keep receipts and source records.
- Reconcile statements on a set cadence and investigate unmatched entries.
- Restrict access to banking, payment, bookkeeping, and tax records; enable strong authentication.
- Record refunds, chargebacks, credits, and deferred/prepaid services explicitly.
- Keep a calendar for filing dates, renewals, contract notice periods, and annual vendor commitments.
- Export data and know how to change providers; protect accounting exports and backups.

## Taxes, records, and jurisdiction

Tax registration, estimated payments, sales/VAT/GST collection, invoicing, deductible costs, records retention, and entity choice vary by location, sales destination, product, and business structure. Do not infer that a payment processor handles all of your obligations. Use the relevant tax authority's current primary guidance and seek local advice when choosing an entity, hiring, selling cross-border, or handling regulated goods/data.

The [U.S. SBA manage-your-business guide](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/manage-your-business/) is a starting point for U.S. small-business bookkeeping, cash-flow, and compliance concepts, not globally applicable tax instructions.
