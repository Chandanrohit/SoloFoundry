# Distribution and launch experiments

**Maturity:** reviewed method guide; no channel or launch campaign has been run for this project.

Distribution is a repeated way to put a useful product in front of the right people. A launch is one event in that system. Pick a channel based on buyer behavior, trust, reach, learning speed, and your ability to maintain it—not because it is currently popular.

## Choose one channel to learn

| Channel | Often useful when | Solo-builder cost to examine |
|---|---|---|
| Direct conversations / targeted outreach | Buyer is identifiable and problem needs explanation | Research time, trust, legal rules for outreach |
| Communities and partnerships | Users already gather around a shared workflow | Participation time, community rules, long trust-building cycle |
| Search content | People actively search for a known problem | Slow compounding, writing and technical maintenance |
| Product-led referrals | A user naturally invites another person into the task | Product must already deliver value; referral incentives can distort behavior |
| Marketplaces/integrations | Buyers choose tools in an established ecosystem | Fees, policy dependence, listing and support work |
| Paid promotion | Conversion path and economics are measurable | Cash risk, attribution noise, creative iteration |

For each, state the target person, reachable source, expected cost, time to learn, and one disconfirming result. Do not commit to several channels before you know what signal would change your decision.

## Design a small experiment

Write down:

- **Hypothesis:** this channel can reach a defined audience with a relevant promise.
- **Action:** the smallest respectful exposure that tests that belief.
- **Measure:** qualified conversations, activated users, retained use, paid conversion, or another downstream outcome—not impressions alone.
- **Window and denominator:** who was exposed, how many, for how long, and what counts as success.
- **Cost:** cash, time, incentives, support burden, and data collection.
- **Stop/continue rule:** threshold and what you will do if results are inconclusive.

Use the [growth experiment template](../../templates/GROWTH_EXPERIMENT.md) and log actuals in the [experiment log](../../templates/EXPERIMENT_LOG.md). Treat small samples as learning signals, not precise conversion estimates. Note selection bias and seasonality.

## Plan a launch around readiness

Before a public announcement, confirm the user can understand the offer, complete the core task, recover from likely failures, get support, and find current terms/privacy information. Check capacity limits, pricing display, signup/onboarding, accessibility, analytics consent/retention, billing behavior, and rollback/feature-disable path. For a checklist, adapt [launch checklist](../../templates/LAUNCH_CHECKLIST.md).

A launch sequence can be: private pilot → address critical issues → invite a broader group → publish a truthful product page → follow up with users who opted into contact → review outcomes and support. Do not announce unverified performance, security, customer counts, savings, or testimonials.

## Content and search

Create content that answers real user questions with original experience, clear sources, and useful examples. Maintain technical basics: pages should be crawlable, internally linked, readable, and accurate. Google states that its AI search features use existing search fundamentals and do not require special “GEO” files or guaranteed optimization tricks; see [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) and [Search Essentials](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials). That is Google-specific guidance; other discovery surfaces may differ.

Measure qualified visits, sign-up/activation, return use, conversion, and support load. Search visibility is delayed and uncertain, so pair it with a faster learning channel. Do not publish large volumes of unoriginal AI-generated pages; invest editorial time in accuracy and distinct value.

## Review cadence

Every two to four weeks, compare channel cost and user outcomes, read a sample of conversations/support notes, and decide whether to continue, change one variable, or stop. Remove experiments that collect data without a decision attached. Protect trust: be transparent about sponsorships, incentives, and outreach, and follow the rules that apply to the market.
