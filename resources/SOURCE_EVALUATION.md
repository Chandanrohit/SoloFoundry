# Source evaluation policy

Prefer sources in this order, depending on the claim: official specifications and documentation; standards bodies and regulators; original research papers and datasets; maintainer-authored repositories and release notes; reputable technical books and courses; experienced practitioner write-ups; community discussions as leads or anecdotal evidence.

## Evaluate a repository
- Is it maintained? Check recent commits, releases, issues and maintainer activity.
- Is its purpose and scope clear?
- Is there a license, and does it permit the intended use?
- Are examples tested and security practices credible?
- Are dependencies and deployment assumptions visible?
- Does it solve the same problem or merely share keywords?
- Are claims supported by code, docs or real usage?

Stars and forks are discovery signals, not proof of quality. A repository may be valuable as a reference without being suitable to adopt.

## Citation record
Record title, author/maintainer, URL, source type, publication/release date, date checked, license, relevant claim and limitations. Prefer linking rather than copying.
